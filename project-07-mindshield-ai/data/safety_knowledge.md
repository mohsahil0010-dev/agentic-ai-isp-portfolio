# ScamShield Safety Knowledge Base

## OTP passwords and recovery codes
Never disclose an OTP, PIN, password, CVV, authenticator code, seed phrase, or recovery code to another person. A legitimate support representative should not need the secret that authorizes a login or transaction. If a secret was disclosed, change the affected credentials from a trusted device, enable multi-factor authentication, and revoke other sessions.

## Payment and bank transfer response
If money was sent, contact the bank or payment provider immediately through its official app, the number on the card, or a website found independently. Ask whether the transaction can be stopped, recalled, frozen, or disputed. Preserve the transaction reference and the complaint number. Do not send another payment to recover the first one.

## Suspicious link response
Do not open a suspicious link to test it. A displayed link may redirect elsewhere, and a familiar-looking domain can still be fraudulent. If already opened, close it, do not download files, do not enter details, and run a trusted security scan. If credentials were entered, change them using the official site or app.

## Independent verification
Verify a request using contact details you obtain independently, not a number or link inside the message. Open the official application yourself or type the known official address. Ask the organization whether the named employee, transaction, parcel, job, or alert is genuine.

## Job and recruitment safety
Be cautious when a job is offered without a proper interview, uses a personal email account, promises unusually high pay, or requests money for registration, training, security, equipment, or a visa. Verify the company and vacancy through its official careers page and published contact details before sharing identity documents.

## Prize lottery and advance-fee safety
An unexpected prize that requires a fee, tax, customs charge, or secret code before release is a strong scam pattern. Do not pay to claim an unsolicited reward. Verify any promotion through the organizer's independently located official channels.

## Investment and crypto safety
Guaranteed profits, fixed daily returns, pressure to act quickly, and requests for cryptocurrency or private-wallet transfers are strong risk signals. Do not rely on screenshots of profits or testimonials. Verify the firm and understand that transfers may be irreversible.

## Remote access and device control
Do not install remote-access applications or begin screen sharing because an unexpected caller or message claims there is a security problem. Remote control can expose banking apps, messages, and saved passwords. End the interaction and contact the organization independently.

## Evidence preservation and reporting
Keep screenshots, full message headers when available, sender details, phone numbers, URLs, account numbers, payment references, and timestamps. Use the platform's report and block controls. For monetary loss, identity theft, threats, or persistent targeting, report through the relevant official provider and national cybercrime channel.

## Privacy and safe use of ScamShield
Do not paste passwords, OTPs, PINs, CVVs, seed phrases, full card numbers, or unnecessary identity-document numbers into an analysis tool. Redact sensitive values first. Automated analysis can miss novel scams and cannot prove that a message is legitimate; important decisions require independent verification.
